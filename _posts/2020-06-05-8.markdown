It's hard to look at a 13.3 percent unemployment rate and be excited about it. But given where we were just a few weeks ago when the jobless rate was 14.7 percent, it's welcomed news. Especially considering many economists expected unemployment numbers to continue rising.

The U.S. economy added 2.5 million jobs in May which is nothing short of incredible. This stunning reversal gives hope to the idea a V-shaped recovery is on the horizon. The numbers also point to a demand in employment as businesses begin to reopen. This is important because many had forecasted sustained unemployment as businesses reopened and tried regaining traction.

Be prepared for market exuberance and a market baking in a recovery before a full economic recovery occurs. Stock prices will begin to rally, and will probably rally a little too much. That said, an excited market may introduce more capital into beaten up stocks which isn't a bad thing in the short-term. The medium-term poses the biggest threat as too many unknowns have the potential for major disruption.
