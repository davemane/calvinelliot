The old saying is true - no one makes good decisions when they're drunk or panicked. Nevertheless, panic ensued on Monday when the Senate failed to approve a stimulus package and the DOW closed down 37% from its all-time high in mid-February. Questions about the economy's viability through COVID-19's grip on a socially-distanced world leaves a lot of unknowns for folks checking their 401(k)s ten times a day.

But it's time to sober up. The reality is, the federal government will step up and inject billions of dollars into the economy. Companies are going to be bailed out and a capital injection will be implemented in the form of checks going directly into the pockets of taxpayers. This is going to lead to a stabilization of the downward spiral markets appear to be headed toward. This does not mean markets will necessarily experience a v-shaped recovery, but it does mean the bleeding will stop. Additionally, it does not mean damage to the economy will be avoided completely. In fact, the economy getting thrashed and GDP taking a hit it hasn't experienced in quite some time is a near certainty.

As always, this too shall pass. People will get back to work and the government isn't going to just walk away from this problem once a stimlus begins to be implemented. This is welcome news for anyone who is remaining calm in this storm. Those with a clear mind understand the fleeting concerns we're facing, and can/should take advantage of good companies being sold at a discount. However, those who are panicked and drunk with fear need to brace for the recovery if they make rash decisions to keep themselves calm in the near-term.

While waiting for the government to announce a stimulus agreement, buy with the assurance of a historically resilient market.
