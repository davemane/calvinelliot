After 9/11, no one was planning on flying again. After Hurricane Katrina, no one wanted to own beach front real estate again. And right now, no one wants to congregate in groups more prone to the spread of coronavirus.

As we've seen in the past, sentiment and concern will shift to convenience and rationality. It is not plausible to assume over the next twelve months airlines will be so stricken with empty planes that they will simply become unprofitable. The same can be said with major cruise lines. While it may seem impossible now, the reality is consumers will weight risks, demand services, and companies will ease restrictions to accomodate the public who has done risk assessment on a personal level.

A couple months ago, I wrote [a post](http://davemane.com/markets/economics/2020/03/22/1.html) on price elasticity and how a few companies may fair. Around that same time it became overwhelmingly obvious unemployment numbers, down revenues, etc. were already priced into the market. I started buying. And I started buying companies with little price elasticity, or companies which have moats, if you will.

Granted, there are a handful of major airliners transporting people around the globe. And there are handful of cruise liners willing to sail passengers around pre-determined routes in luxury for an exchange of cash. But the handful in each of these categories are so well-protected from meaningful long-term damage that I have a hard time seeing them all disappear. That's why I started buying both airlines and cruise lines in March and have been adding to my positions gradually. Specifically, I like American Airlines ([$AAL](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/aal)), Delta Airlines ([$DAL](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/dal)), Royal Caribbean([$RCL](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/rcl)), and Norwegian Cruise Line ([$NCLH](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/nclh)) (this is not legal, tac, investment, or financial advice and should not be construed as such).

In two months, don't look back on this post and wish you had started buying quality companies in these areas. There's risk in the short-term, but there is almost complete certainty for great gains in the long-term.
