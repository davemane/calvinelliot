On June 15th, the DOW closed at 25,763 while the S&P 500 closed at 3,066. Neither set a record at the closing bell, but one thing remains abundantly clear: markets are incredibly disconnected from economic realities.

If you asked anyone six months ago what the stock market would look like if the unemployment rate suddenly quadrupled, they would be at a loss to fully comprehend how deep the pain would stem from gears of the economy grinding to a halt. However, the market is already pricing in a recovery from the COVID recession and the consequences are 1,000-point swings that make day traders giddy.

Seriously, the unemployment rate fell to <i>13.3 percent</i> and you would hardly know it from how well markets are performing. Below is a chart detailing the swings in the DOW Jones Industrial Average and the unemployment rate.

<img src="/assets/rate_dow.png"/>

If there truly is a direct correlation between general economic conditions and stock market performance, we would expect to see an inverse relationship on the chart above. While we see subtle signs of such a relationship, this chart shows the past few months have been a case study proving markets sometimes perform independently and irrationally.
