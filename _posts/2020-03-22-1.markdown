The unfortunate reality of price elasticity will prove humbling to companies which have thrived over the past decade. The seemingly endless bull market hit a brick wall, and since the wreckage is still on fire, first responders haven't been able to fully assess the damage.

Companies like Apple ([$AAPL](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/aapl)), Lululemon ([$LULU](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/lulu)), and Tesla ([$TSLA](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/stock/tsla)) are in for a rude awakening when the smoke begins to clear. However, for most companies, the pain won't last. Apple, which is far from being in actual trouble thanks to being one of the best brands on Earth and having a stockpile of cash, will find momentary trouble selling cell phones (especially with increasing lifespans and staggering pricepoints) in an economy heading for recession. However, being Apple, I'm sure a contingency plan is in place.

Despite a positive outlook by most analysts, Lululemon is also at risk from elasticity in the athleisure space. With a high price point and satisfactory substitutes at cheaper prices, the company's luxury status will take an almost immediate hit as the scope of poor economic conditions comes into focus. Additionally, the priced-in factor relating to positive guidance given back in January (pre-COVID-19) will start being realized when that outlook doesn't come anywhere near to fruition.

Tesla faces a unique problem. They've never truly operated at scale in an economy experiencing a recession. However, with the recent plunge in oil prices along with the mystery behind one of the world's most promising (maybe?) companies, a perfect storm may be avoided and Tesla stock could [stay in orbit](https://www.space.com/spacex-starman-tesla-falcon-heavy-first-orbit-sun.html). Tesla's primary clientele is likely to be uneffected by the uneasy economy, so the automaker will probably end up slipping through the cracks of a sinking stock market and rise to new heights.

Other areas of the market are going to the feel the pain of elasticity. This may prove to be a good time to buy the stocks of quality brands at a discount. We very well may not see prices this low again anytime soon. This is all conjecture, of course. I could be completely wrong and financial markets could very well be melting down and taking every public company down with it.
