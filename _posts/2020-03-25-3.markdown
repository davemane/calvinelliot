A tentative agreenment was reached in the Senate for a massive relief package aimed to offset the descruction caused by COVID-19. The $2 trillion stimulus package is set to come in the form of the following:
<br>

* <b>Direct payments:</b> Americans will receive a one-time direct deposit of up to $1,200, and couples will get $2,400. The payments will be phased out based on income levels (reported in 2018 taxes), starting with individuals earning $75,000 annually and ending with individuals earning $99,000 annually. The same applies for couples earning $150,000 annually to $198,000 annually. Families receive an additional $500 per child.
* <b>Small businesses</b> will get $367 billion to keep making payroll while workers have to stay home. Companies with 500 employees or less that keep paychecks steady could get up to $10 million each in forgivable small business loans. Federally guaranteed loans will provide eight weeks of assistance for qualifying employers who maintain payroll. Those who meet requirements would have costs such as utilities, mortgage interest and rent forgiven.
* <b>The unemployed:</b> The program's extended unemployment insurance program — "unemployment on steroids," as Chuck Schumer calls it — expands eligibility and offers workers an additional $600 per week for four months, on top of what state programs pay. It also allows furloughed workers to stay on as employees, so that when the crisis ends they can quickly resume work. The deal extends to gig economy workers.
* <b>Hospitals and health care workers:</b> The deal injects $130 billion into hospitals and the nation's health system, and billions more into providing personal and protective equipment for health care workers, testing supplies, and increased workforce and training.
* <b>Industry:</b> The final number for hard-hit industries like airlines is still up in the air, but lawmakers are seeking $500 billion in loans - $75 billion in direct help through capital injections and $475 billion for the Federal Reserve to use to leverage loans. That could help stave off layoffs for employees in struggling industries and assure a backstop to loans which can only be done by the Fed. Provisions against potential employer abuses are also still subject to negotiations.
* <b>Payroll taxes:</b> The measure enables individuals to defer payment of their 2020 payroll taxes until 2021 and 2022.
* <b>States and local governments</b> will get $150 billion, with $8 billion set aside for tribal governments.
* <b>Agriculture:</b> The deal would increase the amount the Agriculture Department can spend on its bailout program from $30 billion to $50 billion, according to a press release issued by Sen. John Hoeven (R-N.D.).

Markets saw an immediate relief on strong signals the stimulus package will be voted on, approved, and signed very soon. This welcomed news led to the second straight day of gains. However, it's important to keep in mind the full impact to the economy is nowhere close to being completely felt. What's happening right now is an emotional reaction to the promise of <i>some</i> relief.

While it may be easy to become relaxed and complacent about the heading markets are on, don't lose sight of how vulnerable the economy is. The fragility of the economy is yet to be anything close to fully realized. Don't be surprised one bit when a second stimulus package begins being talked about in the coming months as word of double-digit unemployment begins to circulate.
